# Stakeholder input: worksheet or workshop

First identify the requested deliverable and audience. A worksheet is an independently answerable set of questions; a workshop is a facilitated, collaborative session. Both work as standalone outputs from any folder, including no project folder. They do not depend on the Agentic Experience Hub, a repo decision log, or a persona draft. Drafting first is an optional recommendation when it will make questions more concrete. Do not silently draft or score a persona as preparation.

## Grounding and ownership

Use whatever is supplied: an agent idea, a brief, a partial requirements sheet, a brand guide, a persona draft, or a verbal description. There is no required format or repository file. Extract only the agent concept, intended audience, channel, use cases, stakeholder roles, and explicit choices actually available. An “open decision” is simply a relevant persona question that supplied input has not settled; it need not be tracked anywhere. If the source is partial or absent, frame uncertain context as provisional and ask useful questions; do not convert assumptions into approved design. If essential concept or stakeholder context is missing, ask for that alone; do not require a DRAFT phase, a project folder, or a hub setup. Do not add a brand stakeholder when the user excludes Brand, or assume Legal alone decides product behavior. Organize shared questions and reviewer-specific questions only when multiple groups are requested.

## Worksheet

Use `templates/stakeholder-worksheet-template.md`. Ask the stakeholder to make or frame decisions, with room for an answer, rationale, exceptions, and decision owner when needed. Prefer open, context-specific questions over pre-filled answers and checklists. Include short neutral examples only where they clarify a distinction, and avoid leading pairs that vary task behavior, safety protocol, and tone at once. Link to source evidence only if actually accessible; do not require a persona draft or sample dialog as pre-read. Return the questions inline when requested, with no ancillary file required.

When exact wording is requested, ask whether it governs agent instructions, customer-facing responses, or both; who supplies authoritative text, who approves it, and which scopes require it. Do not assume customer-facing copy is the only immutable surface. Capture explicitly protected instruction text separately for the authoring handoff; do not draft it or decide policy.

For Legal, explore relevant risks and mitigations in the *presentation* of the agent: disclosure and transparency, boundaries on implied agency or expertise, personification and anthropomorphization, humane handling of vulnerable or distressed people, sensitive language, accountability and human oversight, and what wording or examples need review. These are possible areas, not a compulsory list. Let Legal determine whether rules apply, to whom, where, and under which circumstances. Ask who owns approval, any exact prescribed text, and what evidence Legal wants before sign-off. Do not claim a specific disclosure is mandatory or draft legal text. If a question surfaces a policy or safety decision outside Persona, record it as an adjacent-constraint handoff to the appropriate owner without converting it to a voice setting.

### Optional example: high-stakes human-agent assistance

This is an illustration, not a worksheet template. Imagine an assistant supporting an employee during a time-sensitive call. The employee speaks with the caller and remains responsible for decisions. If this context fits the request, a worksheet might briefly establish the assistant's role and audience, ask about character and self-presentation, then compare its wording in a few difficult moments. Use only the parts that help resolve open Persona choices. Select and rewrite questions from supplied decisions, session input, and accessible repository evidence. Do not copy its sections, length, options, or question count; change the setting and questions when the evidence calls for it. An independently answered worksheet can offer answer space and rationale rather than long lists of checkboxes.

Possible prompts, each conditional on the actual use case:

- How should the assistant point out a missed step without blaming the employee? What would that sound like in one short response?
- As urgency rises, how should its words and length change while the human remains responsible for decisions? What should stay constant?
- If showing two short responses, hold the same facts and permitted action constant and vary only expression. Include a write-your-own option. For example, given an unconfirmed detail before closing a call: "Before closing, confirm the missing detail." versus "One detail still needs confirmation before closing." These are wording candidates, not approved procedure or required script.
- Legal-only questions when Legal is a reviewer: Does any word-for-word wording or prohibited language apply to the assistant's presentation? Ask who supplies the authoritative text and who approves it; specify whether it applies to the assistant's words, the employee's words, or both, and record where and when it applies. What review evidence does Legal need for examples and uncertainty phrasing? When should reviewers revisit approved wording, including during a pilot if relevant? Leave cadence open to the reviewer rather than prescribing one.

If a reviewer identifies mandatory steps, escalation authority, safety policy, or operational claims, capture the question and owner as an adjacent-constraint handoff. Do not turn those into Persona settings, assert a protocol, or imply Legal has already approved the example wording. Skip the Legal-only section when no Legal review is requested.

## Workshop

Use `templates/workshop-outline-template.md`. Give a facilitator a runnable collaboration plan: objective, participants and roles, timebox, medium/materials, board layout, activities with prompts and interaction mechanics, how divergent ideas converge into decisions, and how open issues are assigned. In person may use individually completed printed sheets followed by a shared dot-placement spectrum on large paper, whiteboards, poster paper, sticky notes, dry-erase markers, and Sharpies; virtual sessions can use FigJam, Lucid, Miro, or Mural. Give dots enough space, and distinguish placement (a visible distribution of positions) from voting or approval. These are optional mechanics, not a required sequence or a substitute for stakeholder decisions. One workable progression is individual/pair identity input, bounded alternative responses for difficult moments (including a write-your-own option), and a spacious shared scale for a few open dimensions. Preserve each dimension's definition and spectrum in `references/persona-framework.md`; in particular, Personification is distinct from Personality Intensity, Empathy Level is not Brevity, and a name or avatar sketch alone does not establish a personification level. Hold facts and permitted action constant when comparing Persona expression, and hand differences in capability or policy to agent design. The content should not merely be the worksheet questions under "Activity" headings. For a board-ready plan, lay out copy-ready zones and participant input spaces: an optional opening contrast on one caller moment, separate good and bad brand-interaction prompts, one or two scenario moments, a few dimension cards with examples, and a decision area that distinguishes candidate ideas from approval. Select only the zones that serve unresolved choices. Do not copy a source board's dimension count or customer-specific content. Scenario sketches surface expression choices; do not silently expand a Persona workshop into full conversation design. Plan around the stakeholders who will attend, including Legal as an appropriate reviewer when specified, without prescribing legal conclusions.

## Scope and delivery check

Before returning, verify that the primary content matches the request: questions and answer spaces for a worksheet; collaborative mechanics and facilitator instructions for a workshop. Remove author-only dimension/style labels and placeholders from customer-facing output. Do not add a DRAFT status, a completed Persona profile, a linter report, or a HUB to a standalone artifact. Formatting as Google Docs or .docx is the delivery surface's job, not Persona's.
